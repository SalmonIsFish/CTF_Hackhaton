# Cybersecurity Ethics

## Overview

Cybersecurity Ethics means learning and practicing security in ways that are legal, honest, scoped, and respectful. For beginners, the simplest way to approach [[Cybersecurity Ethics]] is to ask what role it plays: does it describe data, a tool, a file, a network behavior, a security idea, or a learning category? Once you know that role, the concept becomes much less mysterious.

A good study habit is to write down what you can directly observe before making assumptions. Many cybersecurity words sound advanced, but they usually point to practical questions: what is this, where did it come from, what does it affect, and what other evidence is connected to it?

## Why it matters in CTFs

In [[CTF]] and security training, [[Cybersecurity Ethics]] matters because it gives beginners vocabulary for authorized testing and defensive improvement. The focus should stay on scope, permission, documentation, impact, and learning. This note avoids offensive procedures and treats the concept as part of ethical education rather than real-world misuse.
