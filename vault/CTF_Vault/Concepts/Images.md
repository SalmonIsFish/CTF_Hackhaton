# Images

## Overview

Images are visual files such as JPEG, PNG, GIF, or BMP. In CTFs, they can contain visible clues, metadata, hidden text, appended data, or steganography. For beginners, the simplest way to approach [[Images]] is to ask what role they play: does the file show something important, describe data, hide a clue, or point toward another artifact? Once you know that role, the concept becomes much less mysterious.

A good study habit is to write down what you can directly observe before making assumptions. Many cybersecurity words sound advanced, but they usually point to practical questions: what is this, where did it come from, what does it affect, and what other evidence is connected to it?

## Why it matters in CTFs

In [[CTF]] forensics challenges, [[Images]] matter because clues and flags are often hidden in ordinary-looking artifacts. A beginner may need to inspect visible text, [[Metadata]], [[EXIF]], embedded data, timestamps, byte patterns, or signs of [[Steganography]]. The useful habit is to preserve the original file, observe carefully, and use tools to answer simple questions about what the artifact contains.

## Related concepts

[[Images]] are closely related to [[EXIF]], [[Metadata]], and [[JPEG]]. These nearby ideas help you place the concept in context instead of memorizing it alone. When a challenge mentions [[Images]], check whether the surrounding evidence points toward files, encoded text, file signatures, hidden text, metadata, or tool output.
