# Digital Forensics

## Overview

Digital Forensics is the careful examination of digital evidence such as files, logs, network traffic, disk images, and memory. For beginners, the simplest way to approach [[Digital Forensics]] is to ask what role it plays: does it describe data, a tool, a file, a network behavior, a security idea, or a learning category? Once you know that role, the concept becomes much less mysterious.

A good study habit is to write down what you can directly observe before making assumptions. Many cybersecurity words sound advanced, but they usually point to practical questions: what is this, where did it come from, what does it affect, and what other evidence is connected to it?

## Why it matters in CTFs

In [[CTF]] forensics challenges, [[Digital Forensics]] matters because clues and flags are often hidden in ordinary-looking artifacts. A beginner may need to inspect file properties, visible text, embedded data, timestamps, packet contents, or byte patterns. The useful habit is to preserve the original file, observe carefully, and use tools to answer simple questions about what the artifact contains.

## Related concepts

[[Digital Forensics]] is closely related to [[Forensics Misc]], [[File Formats]], [[Metadata]]. These nearby ideas help you place the concept in context instead of memorizing it alone. When a challenge mentions [[Digital Forensics]], check whether the surrounding evidence points toward files, web requests, encoded text, logs, packets, permissions, or tool output.
